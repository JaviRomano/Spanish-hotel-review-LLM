How does an AI respond to a hotel's difficult reviews?

A hotel answers hundreds of reviews, and one badly written sentence can admit a fault nobody has verified. This system drafts the reply, checks it and decides whether it is published on its own or goes to a person.

I spent years in hospitality handling guest complaints, and I know how hard it is to do well.

Of the 24 real 1 to 3★ reviews in the test, none was published without review, and that is by design (data). The judge let 2 implicit admissions of fault through (p08 and rag-carteles): these are cases flagged by reading the replies, not a rate.

Why I rebuilt it: 50.8% of the first version's test set was literally in train (reproduction), so that F1 is worthless. On clean data, the classifier reaches a macro F1 of 0.797 on the test set and 0.781 on 11,414 unseen reviews.

What works today and what I have not tested yet
  • It works, locally, on public reviews and synthetic cases: the classifier, context retrieval, the judge and the rules, each measured separately (key figures). Negative, doubtful and trap reviews, and those with accidents or legal claims, always go to a person.
  • What it offers today: it prepares drafts and a person decides; it is not auto-publishing. Only some positive reviews pass every check, and negative ones always go to a person (results).
  • Not tested yet: behaviour with a real hotel's reviews and fact sheets, an acceptable error rate for production, and the work saved. The human evaluation is small (evaluation sets), and the hotels' fact sheets and policies are made up.
What is on this page and how it was made
  • Test reviews: written on purpose to stress the system (legal threats, hidden instructions, false data, attacks on staff), with the criteria for a good reply written before generating and my rating afterwards.
  • Illustrative cases: synthetic cases and real reviews from the Andalusian Hotels' Reviews (AHR) corpus, originally from TripAdvisor, with a note written after reading the reply.

Neither the rules nor the judge are infallible: a person decides anything doubtful or delicate.

Why it was rebuilt

This project comes from auditing the first version. Besides the leak, which came from oversampling before splitting the dataset:

  • Selection on the test set: the model was picked with the test set, with no validation set.
  • No 4★ reviews: “positive” meant 5★ only, while real traffic includes 4★ reviews.
For technical reviewers
  • Out-of-distribution evaluation: the classifier measured on reviews it never saw, not only on the clean test set.
  • Judge against a person: where they agree and how the labels were corrected.
  • Regression and blind comparison: why v6 stays over later versions.
  • ollama_gpu.py: the check that prevents running on the CPU without warning, added after that incident.

Path of a review

  1. The review arrivestext and hotel
  2. ClassifiermRoBERTa (3-seed ensemble) · sentiment and confidence
  3. Hotel fact sheet and policiesfull fact sheet · RAG: policies and approved replies
  4. DraftQwen3.6 27B · aspects as JSON and reply
  5. Rules and judgeGemma 4 12B · 8 criteria with evidence
  6. Gatepublished on its own or goes to a person

The models run on one PC, on a single GPU: no review goes through an external model.

      Known limitations
      
    
        
  • Formulaic replies. Almost all start the same way («Estimado cliente: Muchas gracias por compartir…», Dear guest: Thank you so much for sharing…) and repeat sentences across cases. A prompt version that fixed this lost in a blind human rating. I will try again with more human-rated reviews, so as not to fit the prompt to these same cases.
    • 
        
  • Trap reviews. When it detects hidden instructions or a joke, the reply is deliberately ironic (p02, adv-inyeccion, vampiros). I accept it in context, but the judge fails the tone because it cannot tell this exception apart, and that needs tuning. Traps always go to a person. Pending improvement: a neutral reply when an injection is detected.
    • 
        
  • The judge does not see everything. It lets some implicit admissions of fault through (p08, rag-carteles). That is why negative or doubtful reviews, and any accident or legal risk, always go to a person.
    • 
        
  • Classifier confidence. It is the mean probability of the chosen class across the ensemble's 3 seeds. It almost never exceeds 0.93, and below 0.85 the review goes to a person.
    • 
        
  • The hotel fact sheets, the policies and some of the cases are synthetic.
